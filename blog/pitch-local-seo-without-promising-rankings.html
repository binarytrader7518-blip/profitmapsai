How to Pitch Local SEO Without Promising Rankings

Published 11 November 2026 · 6 min read

Local business owners hear from marketers all the time, and many of those pitches promise "page one" or "#1 on Google". Nobody can honestly guarantee that, and owners have learned to ignore it. The pitches that get answered tend to do the opposite: they show something specific and offer a clear, limited piece of work.

1. Lead with something they can see

A picture of where a business appears on the map, and where it doesn't, is easier to grasp than a list of keyword positions. Keep the first message short: what you checked, what you noticed, and a link to the report.

2. Be specific about the search

Name the keyword and the area. "When someone searches for 'roof repair' on the east side, your business doesn't show in most of the results we checked" is clear and checkable. Vague alarm isn't.

3. Offer work, not outcomes

Describe what you'll do: tidy the Business Profile, fix inconsistent listings, publish regular posts, and build a page for each service area. Price the work. Rankings may improve as a result, but say plainly that they can't be guaranteed.

Avoid: guaranteed positions, fake deadlines, invented competitor claims, or implying you work for Google.

4. Follow up a few times, then stop

Two or three polite follow-ups spaced over a couple of weeks are normal. After that, move on. If you use templates, such as the Pitch Pack included with ProfitMaps AI Gold, edit them so they sound like you and refer to the actual business.

5. Re-scan and report honestly

Once you start the work, run the same scan on the same keyword at regular intervals. Share what improved and what didn't. That honesty is what keeps a client for longer than one month.
